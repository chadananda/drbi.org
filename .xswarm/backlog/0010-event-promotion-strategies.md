## Why one per event, not one campaign
Chad, 2026-09-07: "For DRBI, we need three promotional strategies for each
event — one for each event."

The three differ in kind, so a single campaign would serve none of them. A
weekend study with a named scholar sells on authority. A ten-day immersion over
the holidays sells on commitment and cohort. An unannounced event sells on
nothing until it has a name.

Lead time is the binding constraint. Registration decisions involve travel and
holiday plans, and those lock 6–10 weeks out.

---

## 1. Division, Unity, and the Lord of Books — 26–29 November
Dr. Habib Riazati on the Kitáb-i-Íqán. $340 adult / $200 minor. **~11 weeks out.**

**Hook:** the scholar and the text. Riazati on the Íqán is the reason to come;
lead with substance, not with the venue.

**Shape — authority-led.** Backlog 0009 already has it: 2–3 newsletters on the
overarching theme of teaching. Right instinct. Each should teach something real
so the newsletter has value even to someone who cannot attend — that is what
makes it forwardable.

**The Thanksgiving problem.** It competes directly with family travel. Do not
fight that; frame it as how to spend the holiday — a study retreat as the
Thanksgiving, not instead of it. Families with minors are explicitly priced for
($200), so say so.

**Channels:** newsletter (MailerLite, SPF/DKIM live) → site → **Ampifire**. A
named scholar teaching a dated public course is a legitimate religion-news item,
and the credits are prepaid.

**Timing:** first newsletter now, second at ~6 weeks, third at ~3 weeks.
**Measure:** Humanitix registrations attributed by send date.

## 2. The Dawn-Breakers Challenge 2026 — 25 December to 3 January
Chad Jones and Dr. Nader Saiedi. Week-long reading immersion. **~15 weeks out.**

**Hook:** the commitment itself. This is not a lecture to attend, it is a thing
you undertake — and Saiedi is a serious draw for readers of the Báb's period.

**Shape — cohort-first.** Backlog 0008 already proposes a WhatsApp group and
weekly announcements. That is exactly right, and it should start BEFORE
registration closes, not after: a challenge with a forming group sells itself,
and the group is also the retention mechanism.

**Publish the reading schedule now.** It lets people self-start, and it makes
the commitment concrete rather than abstract. It is also the single most
shareable artefact this event can produce.

**The holiday framing is the advantage here, not the obstacle** — the week
between Christmas and New Year is when people actually have ten days.

**Channels:** WhatsApp cohort → newsletter → **Ampifire**. "Reading the
Dawn-Breakers over the holidays" is a genuine story.

**Timing:** holiday plans lock early — begin now, not in November.
**Measure:** WhatsApp group size as the leading indicator, Humanitix as the real one.

---

## 3. The third programme — NOT YET NAMED
GOAL.md: "Two programmes are listed and a third is coming."

**It cannot be promoted until it has a name, dates, faculty and a price.** That
is the first move, and it is Chad's.

**The strategic argument for defining it NOW rather than later:** the best place
to sell the third event is at the first two. An audience assembled in November
and December is the warmest list this institute will have all year, and it
disperses in January. If the third programme exists by late November it can be
announced to people already in the room; if it does not, that audience is
acquired twice.

## Gate on all three
Standing policy: nothing reaches a public page straight from an Anthropic model,
and **the DeepSeek humanising layer does not exist yet**. Drafting can proceed;
publishing cannot. That layer is the first dependency for every item here.
